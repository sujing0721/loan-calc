How the numbers work

No black box — every figure on this page comes from standard amortization math you can check by hand.

Monthly payment (principal & interest)

M = P × r(1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the amount financed, r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12) and n is the number of months. This is the same formula lenders use.

Mortgage: what's financed vs. what you pay

Loan amount = home price − down payment. The headline number above adds property tax and insurance (your "escrow"), and PMI when the down payment is below 20%. That total is what most borrowers actually send the bank each month.

Auto loan: tax and trade-in

Amount financed = car price + sales tax − down payment − trade-in. Registration and dealer fees aren't included; add them to "car price" if you want them rolled into the loan.

Amortization schedule

Each month, interest = balance × monthly rate, and the rest of your payment chips away at principal. Early on the balance is large, so interest eats most of the payment — the milestone table above shows the crossover.

Questions people ask

What counts as a "good" interest rate right now?
It depends on the loan and your credit. As of 2025–2026, roughly: 30-year mortgages in the mid-6% range, new-car loans around 6–7% for good credit (dealers sometimes advertise lower via incentives), and personal loans anywhere from ~8% to 30%+. Always compare the APR, not just the rate — the APR includes fees.
Why does my mortgage lender quote a higher payment than this?
The quote likely includes escrow items we can't guess exactly — county tax reassessments, higher insurance, HOA dues, or lender-specific PMI tiers. Plug their numbers into the tax/insurance/PMI boxes and the gap usually disappears.
Does paying extra each month actually help?
Yes — every extra dollar goes straight to principal, which shrinks future interest. On a $300k, 30-year loan at 6.8%, an extra $200/month cuts roughly 7 years and tens of thousands of dollars of interest. Add the extra to the "payment" mentally and check the milestone table sooner.
When does PMI go away?
Lenders must automatically cancel it once you hit 80% loan-to-value on the original price, and you can request removal at 80% with a good payment record. If your home has appreciated, a new appraisal can get you there sooner.
Is a 15-year mortgage or a 5-year car loan better?
Shorter terms always cost less in total interest, but the higher monthly payment is real. The usual rule: only take the short term if you'd actually pay it — otherwise a longer term with a bare-minimum payment you never miss beats a "cheap" loan you struggle with.
Is my loan data saved or sent anywhere?
No. Everything runs in your browser with plain JavaScript — the numbers never leave your device and we don't store them. See our privacy policy.
